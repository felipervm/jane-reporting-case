# Turning Clinic Data into Easier Decisions

**Independent product + data case study by Felipe Mattos**  
A research-led look at reporting friction in Jane's public workflows, and how to validate potential improvements before building anything.

## The question

Which recurring clinic-owner reporting questions still involve repetitive setup, exports or external analysis—and which of those workflows are common enough to deserve a simpler product experience?

This is a **hypothesis and validation plan**, not a claim that the problem has been quantified or that Jane should ship a particular feature.

## Research approach

1. **Listen to public customer feedback** on reporting workflows.
2. **Cross-check against Jane's own documentation** instead of assuming capabilities are missing.
3. **Map the task** from recurring clinic question through report filtering, possible export and external analysis.
4. **Propose validation**: talk to clinic operators, assess frequency and time cost, distinguish edge cases from repeated problems.
5. **Recommend only the smallest improvement justified by evidence**, preserving flexibility for advanced spreadsheet work.

## What the case demonstrates

- Product discovery and structured problem framing
- Qualitative research and evidence evaluation
- Workflow mapping and operational reasoning
- Practical prioritization and experiment design
- Communicating findings to technical and nontechnical stakeholders

## Evidence and limitations

The live page links to **public customer feedback on Capterra** and **Jane's Appointments Report documentation**. The signals are exploratory, not representative survey evidence; no internal Jane data, interviews or production analytics were available. The study deliberately does not present fabricated numerical outcomes.
